## Corrections made

- **2026-10-19 · ch 49** Crohn's drugs: natalizumab was listed among the TNF-alpha inhibitors. Now says natalizumab keeps white cells from leaving the blood for the tissues (alpha-4 integrin), as the drug card, breakdown and questions do. The book itself lists it with the TNF-alpha inhibitors and their cautions, so the notes also carry an exam note saying so.
- **2026-10-20 · ch 45** ALT and AST were said to rise much more in hepatitis than in cirrhosis, and Clostridium difficile had been silently renamed. Now the book's grading (a significant rise in hepatitis, moderate in cirrhosis, mild in pancreatitis), and the book's organism name with the current one (Clostridioides) in brackets.
- **2026-10-20 · ch 46** Paraesophageal hiatal hernias were said to need surgery "often" (notes and two practice items); GERD's 18–28% read as if it applied to younger adults; "many" hiatal hernias were asymptomatic; Stretta was "not for obesity". The book favors medical care when possible, with surgery for volvulus, bleeding, obstruction, strangulation, perforation or breathing compromise; 18–28% of North American adults; most are asymptomatic; patients with obesity or severe symptoms may not be candidates.
- **2026-10-20 · ch 47** Gastric-ulcer pain was "not relieved by food"; practice item 47|n|3 called quadruple therapy (with bismuth) triple therapy; the sucralfate drug card said to keep other drugs 2 hours apart. Table 47.2: gastric-ulcer pain is worsened by eating and rarely comes at night (the full table is now in the notes); the item now says quadruple therapy and names the drugs; the card gives Table 47.1's rule (not within 30 minutes of antacids or other drugs) beside the 2-hour spacing drug references use for drugs sucralfate binds.
- **2026-10-20 · ch 48** The overview said alosetron causes ischemic colitis by slowing the gut; the screening bullet said "FIT yearly". Alosetron slows the gut and can cause severe constipation or ischemic colitis (the book's complications, without the causal claim); FOBT or FIT yearly, as the book has it.
- **2026-10-20 · ch 49** Practice item 49|n|0 tied rebound tenderness to perforation. Its rule now gives the book's cue: pain that worsens with coughing or moving and eases when the right hip or knees are flexed; rebound tenderness may also be found.
- **2026-10-20 · ch 53** The aging rule listed the ovaries among the exceptions. The book's one exception is the enlarging prostate; ovarian function still declines, only faster.
- **2026-10-20 · ch 55** The hyperparathyroidism lab row left out vitamin D and the "primary" qualifier on high calcium. Table 55.3 in full: vitamin D variable, calcium high in primary disease, SI units added.
- **2026-10-20 · ch 56** CGM: "some" sensors need two calibrations a day; older adults' exercise sessions of "5–10 minutes"; two insulin Quick bullets (the rapid-acting peak, detemir's duration). The book's versions: most sensors; sessions under 5–10 minutes; peak about 0.5–3 hours; detemir 12–24 hours (degludec 24+).
