## Accuracy

Disagreements and gaps, hub vs book:
- Hyperparathyroidism labs. The hub listed calcium, magnesium and PTH up and phosphorus down, with no vitamin D. The book (Table 55.3) gives vitamin D as **variable** and limits the high calcium to **primary** hyperparathyroidism. Fixed: added both, citing Box 55.7 "CKD with hypocalcemia" for secondary disease.
- Hypothyroid hair. The hub said "thin brittle hair"; Box 55.2 says dry, coarse, brittle hair with decreased growth. Reworded.
- Cinacalcet. The hub gave no indication; the book limits it to more severe symptoms or parathyroid cancer. Added.
- No hub statement contradicted the book on numbers, timings, drugs, priorities or step order.

Book oddities kept:
- "Report a rise of even 1°F (1.8°C)." 1.8 is the conversion factor; a 1°F change is about 0.56°C. The hub had copied it. I kept the book's figure and added an italic note in both places (Monitor item, storm Critical rescue item). **Decision:** keep the note, or drop "(1.8°C)".
- The book prints "saturated solution of potassium iodine [SSKI]". The drug is potassium **iodide**, and the hub already uses the correct name, so I kept it.
- Table 55.3 gives magnesium as increased in hyperparathyroidism and decreased in hypoparathyroidism. Clinically this is arguable (magnesium is often normal), but I kept the book's version. The table prints the critical magnesium units as "mEq/L mg/dL"; the hub uses mEq/L.
- Some of Table 55.1's SI conversions don't compute exactly (e.g., total T4 4–12 mcg/dL ≈ 51–154 nmol/L, but the book prints 59–135). I copied them as printed.

Hub adds (correct general knowledge, kept):
- PTH raises gut calcium absorption through vitamin D.
- Acetaminophen as the non-salicylate antipyretic, because aspirin frees bound hormone.
- Propranolol slows T4→T3 conversion.
- Myxedema coma: passive warming rather than aggressive rewarming; the low sodium is dilutional.
- Secondary/tertiary hyperthyroidism is "rare".
- Seizure precautions for hypocalcemia.
- The "stones, bones, groans, moans" mnemonic.
- Hydration prevents stones.
- Practice rationales: steroids for possible adrenal insufficiency (55|n|6); fetal thyroid takes up iodine from about the end of the first trimester (55|c|12).

New, labeled in the notes as linking two book statements: IV hormone in myxedema coma carries the same cardiac risk as any start of replacement.
