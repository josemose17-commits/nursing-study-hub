## Accuracy

- **Fixed:** aging "The general rule" said "Exceptions: the prostate enlarges… Ovarian function falls faster…". The Quick bullet listed the ovaries under "Exceptions". The book names the prostate as the notable exception; ovarian function still declines, just faster. Reworded both.
- No other disagreements. These all match the book: K⁺ +0.1 mEq/L, cortisol low 12 h after its peak, 85/15 epi/NE, 1–2 million islets, 97°F and 60/min, fluids every 2 h, 2 L, 2-week wound, A1C 100–120 days, scan at 4–6 h.
- **Hub adds (kept, correct):**
  - "formerly diabetes insipidus" for AVP-D/AVP-R.
  - Insulin resistance as the example of a poor receptor response.
  - "Insulin is the only hormone that lowers glucose" (why).
  - Parathyroid injury in thyroid surgery causes hypocalcemia (ch 55).
  - Beta blockers for thyroid tachycardia.
  - Oxytocin "milk let-down" (book: "breast milk secretion").
  - Potential hypoglycemia when insulin is unopposed.
  - Map row "drugs, surgery, radiation".
  - 53|c|8 note that sweating is sympathetic cholinergic.
  - GnRH as the driver of FSH/LH in the new Gonads box (implied by Table 53.1's name; standard physiology).
- **Book oddities kept:**
  - Hysterectomy causes "immediate surgical menopause" (strictly, removing the ovaries does).
  - A1C reflects 100–120 days.
  - NE "mainly alpha", epinephrine "mainly beta" (a simplification).
  - Scan imaged at 4–6 h.
  - "Wound not healed in 2 weeks or less", read as within 2 weeks.
- **Book oddities not carried into the notes:**
  - Table 53.3 calls FSH "interstitial cell– or Sertoli cell–stimulating hormone" in males (ICSH is normally LH).
  - The prostate "producing… higher concentrations of adrenal steroids".
  - The pineal "in the corpus callosum".
  - The health-equity line that T2DM occurs when "the pancreas… cannot effectively use insulin".
  - Fig 53.3's caption says "positive and negative" feedback but shows only negative.
  - The text file repeats Table 53.2's fat, liver and pancreas rows (an extraction duplicate).
