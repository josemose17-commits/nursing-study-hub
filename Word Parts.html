Make the words make sense.

Prefixes modify meaning. Roots carry the core idea. Suffixes describe a condition, process or procedure. A combining vowel such as o joins parts.

Drug naming stems are a separate system. Brand names and abbreviations need a different explanation. Entries without a verified split are labeled; that label does not mean they have no roots.

How to use this reference

Read the clinical definition first. Then cover the breakdown, explain the parts aloud, and check yourself. A literal translation is a memory clue; it cannot establish a diagnosis or tell you a medication’s dose, duration or safety.

The Exam 3 filter means a term appears in those chapters’ existing notes or guided lessons; it is not a separate professor-assigned topic list. “All shared vocabulary” also includes Exam 2 and Final terms.

Definitions reuse the existing study glossary. Curated segmentations name every part actually used, including joining vowels and grammatical endings. Unannotated words remain searchable and are explicitly marked. Drug stems are applied only to listed, checked generic names; the renderer does not guess a class from arbitrary letters.
