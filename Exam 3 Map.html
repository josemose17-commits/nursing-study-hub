Glucose and A1C

Fasting glucose
normal 74–100 · prediabetes 100–125 · diabetes ≥126 mg/dL
A1C
<5.7% normal · 5.7–6.4% prediabetes · ≥6.5% diabetes · target <7%
2-h OGTT · random
≥200 = diabetes · random >200 with symptoms
Home targets
before meals 80–130 · after meals <180 · CGM time in range 70–180, goal 70%
Hospital
before meals <140, random <180 · ICU 140–180 · pre-op <200
Hypoglycemia
<70 → 15 g fast carb, recheck in 15 min · <50 → 30 g

A1C is the last ~120 days of glucose stuck to hemoglobin: A1C 7 ≈ average 154, 8 ≈ 183, 9 ≈ 212. It isn't changed by what was eaten yesterday.

DKA vs HHS

DKA
glucose >300 · ketones + · pH <7.35 · HCO₃⁻ <15 · sudden
HHS
glucose >600 · osmolarity >320 · no ketones · pH >7.4 · gradual
Osmolarity
normal 270–300 mOsm/L · coma above 350
DKA resolved
glucose <200 · HCO₃⁻ >18 · pH >7.30 · anion gap <12
Fluids
DKA 15–20 mL/kg first hour · D5½NS when glucose reaches 250
Potassium
3.5–5.0 · replace once <5.0, only if urine ≥30 mL/hr · bicarbonate only if pH ≤7.0

Fluids before insulin: insulin pulls glucose — and water and potassium — into the cells, so it can drop the BP and the potassium fast.

Thyroid

TSH
0.3–5 μU/mL
Free T4
0.8–2.8 ng/dL
Total T4
4–12 (M) · 5–12 (F) mcg/dL
T3
70–205 ng/dL (age 20–50) · 40–180 (over 50)
Primary hypothyroid
TSH high · T3/T4 low
Graves
TSH low · T3/T4 high · TRAb +

TSH moves opposite to thyroid hormone when the gland itself is the problem: the pituitary shouts when hormone is low and goes quiet when it is high.

Calcium and parathyroid

Calcium (total)
9.0–10.5 mg/dL · above 12 → confusion, psychosis, coma
Phosphorus
3–4.5 mg/dL (critical <1)
Magnesium
1.3–2.1 mEq/L (critical <0.5 or >3)
PTH · vitamin D
10–65 pg/mL · 25–80 ng/mL
Sodium
136–145 mEq/L

Calcium and phosphorus move opposite: low PTH = low calcium, high phosphorus (tingling, tetany); high PTH = high calcium, low phosphorus (stones, bones, groans).

GI and liver labs

ALT · AST
4–36 · 0–35 units/L · highest in hepatitis
Alkaline phosphatase
30–120 units/L · ↑ biliary obstruction, cirrhosis
Bilirubin
total 0.3–1.0 · direct 0.1–0.3 (↑ obstruction) · indirect 0.2–0.8 (↑ hemolysis) mg/dL
Albumin · ammonia
3.5–5.0 g/dL · ammonia 10–80 (6–47 μmol/L)
Amylase · lipase
60–120 Somogyi units/dL (30–220 units/L) · 0–160 units/L · ↑ in acute pancreatitis
CEA · CA 19-9
<5 ng/mL · 0–37 units/mL · track treatment and recurrence, not diagnosis

The liver turns ammonia into urea and makes albumin, so a failing liver shows high ammonia and low albumin. Bile that can't drain backs up as direct bilirubin and alkaline phosphatase; red cells breaking down too fast raise indirect bilirubin.

Bowel: infection, fluids and screening

WBC
normal 5000–10,000 · appendicitis 10,000–18,000 with a shift to the left · >20,000 → perforation? · peritonitis about 20,000
Fever lines
obstruction >100°F with a rising pulse → strangulation · >101°F → perforated appendix, admit for diverticulitis, report after surgery or in UC
Potassium
3.5–5.0 mEq/L · lost with vomiting, NG suction, diarrhea and fistulas (magnesium too)
UC severity
mild <4 stools/day · moderate >4 · severe >6 bloody · fulminant >10 bloody
Colorectal screening
from age 45 · FIT or gFOBT yearly · stool DNA every 3 years · colonoscopy every 10 · sigmoidoscopy or CT colonography every 5
Fiber · ostomy
diverticulosis 25–35 g/day · empty the pouch at ⅓–½ full · change the system every 3–7 days · new ileostomy >1 L/day → add ≥500 mL fluid

An inflamed or blocked bowel leaks fluid inward, into its own lumen and the peritoneum, so a patient can be dry with nothing visible lost. That's why daily weight is the most reliable fluid measure, and why a rising pulse, falling urine output and new confusion in an older adult are early warnings.

Call the provider now · the findings you act on before anything else▾

After a scope (EGD, colonoscopy, ERCP)

RR <10 or low exhaled CO₂
oversedation → sternal rub, airway, reversal per protocol
Severe pain, rigid abdomen
perforation → NPO, vital signs, report now
Pulse rising, BP falling, large bloody or maroon stool
bleeding after polypectomy → report now
Fever, chest or neck pain after EGD
esophageal perforation → NPO, report
Asking to drink
check the gag reflex first — nothing by mouth until it is back

Cramping, gas and a sore throat are expected. Rigidity, a rising pulse and fever are not.

Mouth, esophagus and reflux

Stridor, drooling, swelling after oral or neck surgery
airway → rapid response, stay, suction ready
Burning chest pain spreading to the jaw or arm
treat as cardiac until proven otherwise → ECG, report
Fever, fast pulse and breathing, confusion 2–10 days after esophagectomy
anastomotic leak → surgeon and rapid response now; nothing by mouth
Coughing with meals, wet voice, new crackles
aspiration → upright, NPO, report
NG tube moved after fundoplication or esophagectomy
don’t push it back — call the surgeon

Airway first: anything that narrows the throat beats pain, nutrition or teaching.

Ulcer and upper GI bleeding

Coffee-ground or red vomit, black tarry stool
active or recent bleeding → vital signs, 2 large-bore IVs, report
HR rising, BP falling, urine <30 mL/hr
hypovolemic shock → fluids, type and crossmatch, report now
Sudden severe pain, boardlike abdomen
perforation → NPO, report now, prepare for surgery
Hgb dropping between draws
ongoing bleeding → report the trend
No NG drainage, fullness, hiccups after gastric surgery
tube blocked → call the surgeon; don’t reposition or irrigate without an order

A BUN that rises with a normal creatinine is digested blood. The pulse rises before the BP falls.

Thyroid

High fever, racing pulse, agitation, vomiting in Graves
thyroid storm → report now; cool (acetaminophen, not aspirin); expect methimazole or PTU, iodine after it, propranolol, steroids
RR <12, temp <95 °F, hypotension, stupor in hypothyroidism
myxedema coma → airway, warm slowly, IV levothyroxine per order
Any temperature rise in hyperthyroidism
report — it can be the start of storm
Chest pain or palpitations after starting levothyroxine
dose too fast for the heart → report
Sedatives or opioids in hypothyroidism
expect smaller doses; watch breathing closely

Both emergencies come from a trigger — infection, surgery, stopping the drug — in a client whose thyroid problem isn’t controlled.

After thyroid or parathyroid surgery

Stridor, neck tightness, swelling, blood behind the neck
hematoma or laryngospasm → rapid response, stay, trach tray and suction at the bedside
Tingling of lips or fingers, Chvostek or Trousseau +
low calcium → report, check calcium, IV calcium gluconate ready
Calcium falling between draws
report the trend — it often drops on days 1–3
Voice getting weaker or more hoarse
nerve injury → report (steady mild hoarseness is common)

Low calcium makes nerves fire too easily — from tingling to tetany to a larynx that spasms shut.

Diabetes

Glucose <70, or symptoms you can’t check
treat now: 15 g fast carb if alert (30 g if under 50); glucagon or IV dextrose if not
Low glucose on a sulfonylurea or long-acting insulin
it comes back → recheck often, report
Glucose >250–300 with ketones, Kussmaul breathing, vomiting
DKA → report now, fluids first
Glucose >600, confused, very dry, older client
HHS → report now, fluids
K⁺ <3.5 during DKA treatment
report now — current adult consensus delays/pauses insulin while potassium is replaced until above 3.5
Glucose about 250 on an insulin drip, gap still open
expect dextrose added to the fluids; don’t stop the insulin
New foot wound, redness, black area
same-day referral

When it’s low or high and you can’t tell, treat it as low — a little sugar won’t hurt a high glucose, and a low one can cause seizures.

Older adults

New confusion
often the first sign of dehydration, infection or a low glucose → check vital signs, glucose and intake; report
Mild pain with fever or a fast pulse
older adults can perforate or have peritonitis with little pain → report
A new drug cleared by the liver
watch for toxicity — the aging liver clears drugs more slowly

In older adults, serious problems often look mild or atypical: little fever, little pain, confusion instead.
