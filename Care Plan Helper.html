How to interview your patient

    Find a NANDA-I diagnosis

    All 277 labels from NANDA-I 2024–2026 (13th edition), with their code and the page to read in your book. About 60 of the ones used most on med-surg, surgical and mental health units have a plain-words guide here: the cues to look for, related factors, goals, interventions with rationales and an evaluation.

    How to write the statement

    Problem-focused (it’s happening now):[Label] related to [cause] as evidenced by [what you saw and measured]

    Risk (it could happen):Risk for [label] related to [risk factors] No “as evidenced by”: there are no signs yet.

    Health promotion (the patient wants to do better):Readiness for enhanced [label] as evidenced by [what the patient says or does]

    • The related factor must be something nursing can change. A medical diagnosis (pneumonia, heart failure) is an associated condition, not a related factor.
    • “As evidenced by” uses your patient’s real data: SpO₂ 91% on 2 L, not “low oxygen.”
    • Prove it: the label should be the one your data supports, not the one that sounds closest.
    • Priority: airway, breathing, circulation, then safety, then the rest of Maslow. An actual problem usually comes before a risk.

    My syllabus care plan

    Your entered answers go into the Clinical Reasoning Plan of Care, syllabus pp. 34–47: the same section order and questions, landscape pages, all lab rows, and the five-column care-plan table with separate goals. Answers expand, so page breaks can differ from the blank syllabus.

    Turn my completed form into a Google Doc

    Review your answers, choose your permitted Google account, then create an editable Google Doc with your information already in the form. The saved document is a copy; later changes on the site do not update it.

    Word download and manual Google Docs import
    1. Download the care plan using the Word button above.
    2. In your permitted Google Drive account, choose New → File upload and select the downloaded .docx file.
    3. Open the uploaded file with Google Docs. You can edit the Word file there. To make a native Google Doc, choose File → Save as Google Docs.
    4. Check table widths, page breaks and your instructor’s requirements before submitting.

    This is an alternative to the Google Doc button. Follow your school and clinical site’s rules for information stored in Google Drive.
