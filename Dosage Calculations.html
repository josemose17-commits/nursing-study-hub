NRS 114 · clinical · dosage

Dosage calculations

Your syllabus requires 90% on the proctored dosage calculation exam before you can give medications in clinical — three tries, with remediation before each retake. Learn each topic and check yourself, then drill fresh problems until the setups are automatic, and take practice exams until 90% is routine.

Drill

New numbers every time. Miss one and you get a hint first; miss again and the worked setup follows. Rounding: under 1 mL to hundredths, 1 mL or more to tenths, drops to whole numbers — each question says how.

Practice exam

Mixed calculations, one try each, no hints — like the real one. Pass mark 90%. Use the same calculator you’ll have on the exam, and write each setup on paper.

Rewritten from your dosage calculation notes (eight topics); two slips in the copy are fixed (1 mL = 1 cc; never write “mL.” with a period). Writing rules: ISMP’s List of Error-Prone Abbreviations and The Joint Commission’s Do Not Use list. Insulin times cross-checked with your med-surg book (Ignatavicius, Table 56.6) and IV fluids with Table 13.3. Drug strengths and the reconstitution directions in the drill are typical U.S. labels — always use the label in your hand. Practice problems only: give medications only from a current order, by your agency’s policy.
