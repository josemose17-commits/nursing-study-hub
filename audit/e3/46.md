## Accuracy

Disagreements fixed:
1. **GERD prevalence.** The hub's `Definition` put "(18–28% of North American adults)" right after "increasingly in younger adults", which reads as if 18–28% are younger adults. The book says 18–28% of all North American adults, and separately that the affected population is getting younger. I reworded it.
2. **Paraesophageal hernia treatment.** The overview pair said "→ often surgery". The book says types II–IV are treated by type, severity and complication risk, that medical management is favored when possible, and that surgery is for volvulus, bleeding, obstruction, strangulation, perforation or respiratory compromise. I reworded the pair and the hiatal `Treatment` item.
3. **Box 46.8 context.** The hub said "Many are asymptomatic"; the book says "Most". Fixed, and I added that symptoms worsen after meals or lying supine.
4. **Stretta.** The hub said "Not for obesity or severe symptoms"; the book says such patients "may not be candidates". Softened.
5. **Equity term.** The hub said "professional medical interpreters"; the book says "professional medical translators". I kept the book's word with "(interpreters)" (see book oddities).

Hub adds (correct general knowledge, kept):
- "Rule out the heart first" for GERD-like chest pain.
- "Not just extra pillows" for raising the head of the bed.
- Viscous lidocaine numbs the gag reflex.
- Adenocarcinoma arises where Barrett's develops.
- The box-level `why` texts on PPIs and calcium, and on acid vs alkali chemistry.
- My line linking dehydration to dry mucosa. NGN 46.1 tests it and Box 46.1 says to stay hydrated.

Book oddities kept:
- Nystatin suspension is listed as "swish and spit". Many references say swish and swallow for oral candidiasis. The hub follows the book.
- "Translators" is used where interpreters (spoken language) are meant.
- Stretta is described as working by "decreasing vagus nerve activity".
- The book writes "AIDS [HIV-III]". The hub says AIDS.
