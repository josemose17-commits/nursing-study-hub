NCSBN Clinical Judgment Measurement Model

The six clinical judgment steps

The NCLEX measures how you think through a client’s situation, in six steps. They line up with the nursing process you already know. Each unfolding case study on the exam asks one question for each step, in this order.

    Use the steps as a strategy. Ask which step the question is on. If it asks what to do first and you haven’t recognized and analyzed the cues yet, the answer that gathers data usually wins. In an emergency the answer is action, not more data.
    NCLEX-RN test plan (2026)

    How the exam is built

    • Computer adaptive. Each question is picked from how you answered the last ones. You can’t skip or go back — which also means you can’t talk yourself out of a right answer.
    • 85 to 150 questions in up to 5 hours, breaks included. The exam stops when it is confident you are above or below the passing standard.
    • Three unfolding case studies of 6 questions each come within the first 85 questions — one question per clinical judgment step. Stand-alone NGN items (bow-tie and trend) and traditional items fill the rest.
    • 15 pretest questions aren’t scored, and you can’t tell which ones they are — so treat every question as if it counts.
    • No penalty for guessing. When you have to guess, make it an educated guess with the strategies below.
    • A candidate who doesn’t pass gets a report with a score for each of the six clinical judgment steps. The Mastery test report shows you the same thing after every test.

    Reading the question

    Strategic words to watch for

    Ranking: first, initial, immediate, next, best, most, most important, most likely, priority, primary, essential, early, late.

    Assessment: assess, check, collect, determine, find out, gather, identify, monitor, observe, recognize.

    Negative queries: needs further teaching, needs follow-up, unsafe, contraindicated, least, avoid, except.

    Every question has three parts: the event (the client and situation), the event query (what is being asked about that event) and the options. Read the query twice. Many wrong answers are true statements that don’t answer the query.

    Eliminating options

    Process of elimination, every time: read every option, cross out what is wrong for this client, then read the query again before you choose. On select-all items, judge each option on its own as true or false for this client.

    Communication, pharmacology, math and resources

    Pharmacology points to carry in

    • The exam names drugs by generic name. Learn the name endings that give away the class (-olol beta blocker, -pril ACE inhibitor, -prazole PPI, -tidine H₂ blocker, -gliptin DPP-4 inhibitor, -glutide GLP-1 agonist, -gliflozin SGLT2 inhibitor).
    • From the class, work out the action, the common side effects and what the nurse checks before giving it (pulse, BP, potassium, glucose, a level).
    • Know which effect the question is asking for: the intended effect, a side effect, an adverse effect or a toxic effect.
    • Don’t give a drug when the prescription is unclear, hard to read, or the dose isn’t a normal one — clarify first.
    • For calculations, use the on-screen calculator, round only at the end and only as the question says, and ask whether the answer is a reasonable dose.
