## Accuracy

**Disagreements fixed**
- CGM calibration. Hub: "some need 2 calibrations a day." Book: "most" sensors need ≥ 2 capillary readings a day, and with older systems insulin is given only after confirming. → Changed to the book's version. This is arguably dated, since many current CGMs are factory-calibrated, but the exam follows the book.
- Older adults' exercise sessions. Hub: "(5–10 min)." Book: "less than 5–10 minutes." → Changed to "under 5–10 min".
- Insulin Quick bullets:
  - The rapid-acting peak "~1–3 h" left out lispro's 0.5 h start → now "~0.5–3 h".
  - The long-acting duration "~24 h" contradicted detemir's 12–24 h in the text → now "12–24 h (degludec 24+)".
- BP targets (not an error, but a confusion risk). The cardiovascular section gives < 140/90, with 120/80 for younger adults if reachable without excessive burden. The kidney section gives < 140/80. → Both items now say which section gives which number.

**Hub adds (correct general knowledge, kept)**
- Notes:
  - Metformin rarely causes lows on its own.
  - Glucose rises when insulin is missing during exercise (mechanism).
- Practice items:
  - 0.9% saline as the first DKA fluid (the book gives only the rate).
  - Check glucose around 2–3 a.m. for dawn vs Somogyi (the book says "during the night").
  - Treat a low on acarbose with glucose, not sucrose.
  - Sulfonylureas close K⁺ channels.
  - Beta blockers spare sweating.
  - Check glucose before bed after alcohol.
  - A pump interruption can cause DKA within hours.
- New this pass, labeled *(not in this chapter)*:
  - The clear-before-cloudy order for mixing regular and NPH (the book states no order).
  - Normal capillary refill is under about 3 s.
- Also new this pass:
  - "Each 1% ≈ 28–29 mg/dL" is arithmetic from Table 56.4.
  - The upper arms, thighs and buttocks as injection areas: the text file has only Fig. 56.7's caption, so the figure's content could not be checked. These are the standard sites.

**Book oddities (kept, or not presented as fact)**
- Box 56.8: "Prolonged capillary filling time (greater than 25 seconds)."
  - Almost certainly a misprint: normal refill is under about 3 s. It may be a lost decimal point (2.5 s) or a mix-up with venous filling time.
  - The hub says "prolonged capillary refill" and flags the printed number as doubtful.
- Fig. 56.1 gives fasting normal as 74–106 (it is also the range the book's questions quote), while Table 56.3 gives 74–100. Both are now in the notes, explained.
- The alt text of Fig. 56.1 and Fig. 56.10 labels glucose in "microgram per deciliter"; it should be mg/dL.
- Box 56.4 writes A1C ">6.5%" and random glucose "greater than 200". The text says "two results greater than 126". Table 56.3 and the ADA use ≥ 6.5 and ≥ 126. The hub follows Table 56.3 (≥ 6.5, ≥ 126) and the book's "random > 200".
- Unit typos:
  - "Random glucose > 200 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L)" should be 11.1 mmol/L.
  - DKA resolution "glucose < 200 mg/mL" should be mg/dL.
  - Box 56.11 "< 50 mg/dL [2.2 mmol/L]" should be 2.8 mmol/L.
  - Metabolic syndrome "90 mg Hg" should be mm Hg.
- Ketone-check thresholds disagree:
  - DKA teaching says "> 250 mg/dL (16.7 mmol/L)", but 16.7 mmol/L is 300 mg/dL.
  - The pump section says "> 300 (16.7)".
  - The hub keeps 250 for illness teaching and 300 for pumps, as the book does.
- NCLEX Challenge 56.2 quotes an A1C normal range of 4–5.9%; Table 56.3 says normal is 4–5.7% and prediabetes is 5.7–6.4%.
- Metabolic syndrome uses BP ≥ 140/90 (older than the ≥ 130/85 criterion). Kept, as the caller settled.
- The book says proinsulin is converted "in the liver" (physiologically, this happens in the beta cell) and that glucagon releases glucose from "skeletal muscle" (muscle glycogen is not released into the blood). Neither is in the hub, and neither is exam-relevant.
- "Get Ready… Essential Nursing Care Points": the extracted text has the heading and no points.
