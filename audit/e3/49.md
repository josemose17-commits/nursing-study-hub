## Accuracy

**Disagreements corrected:** none. No hub statement contradicted the book: numbers, ranges, timings, drug names and priorities all match.

**Imprecision tightened:**

- "Otherwise treat as outpatients" (amebiasis) now names the book's group: asymptomatic, mild or moderate disease.
- "Handwashing is the best prevention" was only in the box's hub-written `why`; it is now a book fact in the Action Alert item.

**Book oddities kept:**

- **Hypertonic IV fluids for peritonitis.** This is the book's wording, and it is kept in the 'Fluid balance' text as instructed. It is arguably dated, because isotonic crystalloids are standard for third spacing. That item's Quick bullet already said only "IV fluids". I left it unchanged (your decision).
- **Natalizumab grouped with the TNF-alpha inhibitors.** The book says these drugs inhibit TNF-alpha and are not given with a history of cancer, heart disease or MS. The hub keeps the correct mechanism (it blocks white cells from entering tissue) and now adds an exam note that the book groups them. A further oddity: natalizumab is itself an MS treatment, so the book's "not with MS" caution doesn't fit it. The note reports the book's grouping without endorsing that caution. The overview "Why the drug makes sense" pair and the drug cards still give only the true mechanism.
- **J-pouch stage 2 timing.** The text says within 1–2 months; the Fig. 49.2 caption says "usually several months". The hub follows the text.

**Hub adds (correct, kept):**

- Barley and rye as gluten sources (49|c|3 and an existing Why card); the book names wheat.
- "Hold the next dose" for new neuro signs on natalizumab (49|c|0); the book says report immediately.
- Overview act/map line "pain that eases then becomes diffuse = perforation"; the book has this only in the NGN case.
- The physio/why explanations throughout.

**Existing practice imprecision (not edited, because practice is append-only):** the rule in 49|n|0 says "Rebound tenderness and pain eased by flexing the right hip suggest perforation". The book ties perforation to pain that is worse with cough or movement and relieved by bending the right hip or knees. Rebound tenderness is a general sign of peritoneal irritation.
